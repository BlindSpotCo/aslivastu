# AsliVastu — User Feedback (NRI Investor Perspective)

**Context:** Based in the US, looking to buy an investment/eventual-retirement property in Delhi NCR. Can't do site visits myself — relying on family, a broker I don't fully trust yet, and tools like this to narrow down where to even send someone to look. Used AsliVastu to shortlist PINs across South Delhi and Noida before making any calls.

## What worked

For someone who can't physically walk the street, a number from what looks like a government-sourced pipeline (CPCB for air, official crime and infra references) is genuinely reassuring — it's less biased than my relatives, who all insist their own neighborhood is the best one, and less commission-driven than a broker's pitch. The schools section helped me sanity-check a broker's claim about a "top school nearby" against an actual list. Having one composite score to email to my spouse and parents for a quick "does this pass the sniff test" was useful too.

## Where it fell short

**I have no way to independently verify anything, and the site doesn't help me build trust in it.** Living abroad, I can't drive by and check if the "infra_score_raw" or crime figures feel right, the way a local buyer could. That means I'm depending entirely on the tool's credibility, but there's no source citation, no confidence indicator, and no indication of when data was last verified. For a decision this large, made without a site visit, that's a real gap.

**No sense of trajectory — I'm investing for 5-10 years out, not today.** A static snapshot score doesn't tell me whether an area is improving (new metro line, upcoming development) or declining. I'm making a bet on the future of a neighborhood I can't personally monitor, and the tool only gives me the present.

**Nothing connects livability to investment return.** I don't just want to know if an area is pleasant — I want rental yield potential, price appreciation trends, and vacancy/demand signals, especially since I'd likely rent this property out rather than live in it myself. The current scope stops at "quality of life," which is only half of what an investor needs.

**Doesn't help with the trust problem I actually came here with.** My biggest risk as an NRI isn't neighborhood quality — it's fraud, disputed title, unauthorized construction, or a broker misrepresenting a property. This tool doesn't touch any of that, and I'd have appreciated it flagging what it does and doesn't cover so I don't mistake "neighborhood score" for "this deal is safe."

**Time zone and remote workflow weren't considered.** Nothing wrong technically, but there's no way to save a shortlist, share it with a broker or family member with commentary, or get any kind of alert if a score changes on a PIN I've bookmarked. I'm coordinating this across three time zones and multiple people, and right now I'd have to redo my own research each time I check back.

**Currency and non-local framing.** Not critical, but AQI categories and government-report-style jargon assume a reader who already lives in Delhi. As someone who's been away for a while, a plain-language translation ("comparable to a bad wildfire-smoke day in the US") would help me calibrate faster.

## Suggestions, in order of what would help most

1. Add a clear "how this is calculated / how current is this" disclosure per dimension — trust is the entire value proposition for someone who can't verify locally.
2. Show trend direction (improving/declining/stable) per dimension, not just a current snapshot.
3. Add a basic rental yield or price trend layer, even directional, since most NRI buyers here are investing, not relocating immediately.
4. Let me save/share a shortlist with notes, so I can coordinate with family and my broker without starting over each session.
5. Be explicit about scope — state clearly that this scores neighborhood livability only, not property-level legal or title risk, so I don't over-trust it in areas it doesn't cover.

## Bottom line

As a starting filter to avoid embarrassingly bad picks sight-unseen, it's useful and better than relying on word-of-mouth alone. But for someone making a six- or seven-figure decision from another country with no ability to verify locally, I need more transparency about the data's trustworthiness and more investment-relevant signals before I'd treat this as more than a first pass.
